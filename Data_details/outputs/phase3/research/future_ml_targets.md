## Executive Summary

Phase 3 established that classical digital signal processing effectively removes high-frequency road chatter, engine harmonics, and impulse spikes ($> 80\%$ high-frequency energy reduction). However, **no linear or classical filter can solve low-frequency sensor bias drift or gravity leakage**, which inherently cause open-loop double integration of acceleration to diverge quadratically ($e_p(t) \propto \frac{1}{2} a_{\text{err}} t^2$).

To achieve ISRO's requirement of **$< 10\%$ positional drift** over extended GNSS outages (up to 120 seconds), **Phase 4 must introduce AI/ML models that estimate motion constraints directly**, bypassing classical unconstrained double integration ($a \to v \to p$).

This document formally specifies:
1. Input tensor representation derived from Phase 3 conditioned signals.
2. Five candidate learning targets extractable from the IO-VNBD dataset.
3. Comparative analysis of target formulations (pros, cons, error accumulation).
4. Recommended target hierarchy for Phase 4 neural network training.

## 1. Input Tensor Formulation (Phase 3 Handoff)

The neural network in Phase 4 receives conditioned temporal sliding windows generated by `Data_details/src/phase3_pipeline.py`:

$$\mathbf{X}_k \in \mathbb{R}^{W \times F}$$
- **Window Length ($W$)**: $30\text{ samples}$ ($3.0\text{ seconds}$ at $10\text{ Hz}$). Captures complete driving maneuvers (e.g. onset, peak, and recovery of braking or lane changing).
- **Stride ($S$)**: $5\text{ samples}$ ($0.5\text{ seconds}$, $83.3\%$ overlap) for training; $1\text{ sample}$ ($100\text{ ms}$) for causal real-time inference.
- **Feature Dimension ($F = 12$) [Architectural Upgrade]**:
  1. $a_{\text{fwd}}$: Filtered forward vehicle-frame dynamic acceleration (m/s²)
  2. $a_{\text{lat}}$: Filtered lateral vehicle-frame dynamic acceleration (m/s²)
  3. $a_{\text{vert}}$: Filtered vertical vehicle-frame dynamic acceleration (m/s²)
  4. $\omega_{\text{roll}}$: Vehicle-frame roll rate (rad/s)
  5. $\omega_{\text{pitch}}$: Vehicle-frame pitch rate (rad/s)
  6. $\omega_{\text{yaw}}$: Vehicle-frame yaw rate (rad/s)
  7. $\Delta a_{\text{fwd}} / \Delta t$: Longitudinal jerk rate (m/s³)
  8. $\|\mathbf{a}_{\text{horiz}}\|$: Horizontal acceleration magnitude $\sqrt{a_x^2 + a_y^2}$ (m/s²)
  9. $\|\boldsymbol{\omega}\|$: Total angular velocity norm (rad/s)
  10. $\theta$: Pitch tilt angle from Complementary Filter (deg)
  11. Spectral Vibration Ratio: Ratio of high-frequency energy ($\ge 1.8\text{ Hz}$) to total energy
  12. `is_stationary`: Instantaneous binary ZUPT flag ($1.0$ if stopped, $0.0$ if moving)

All features are pre-filtered with our **Causal Butterworth 1.5 Hz + Hampel** pipeline and aligned with the vehicle body frame, guaranteeing that high-frequency vibration noise and impulse outliers cannot corrupt the neural network weights.

```
                   IO-VNBD GROUND TRUTH SENSORS

            └──────────┬──────────┴──────────┬──────────┘
                       │                     │
                       ▼                     ▼
     ┌────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┐
     │                CANDIDATE TARGET TAXONOMY                │
     ├────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┤
     │  TARGET A: Forward Speed (v_fwd)                       │
     │  TARGET B: 2D Velocity Vector [v_east, v_north]        │
     │  TARGET C: Relative Displacement [Δx, Δy]              │
     │  TARGET D: Yaw Heading Rate (Δψ / dt)                  │
     │  TARGET E: Residual Acceleration Correction (δa)       │
     └────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┘
```

### Target A: Forward Vehicle Speed ($v_{\text{fwd}}$)

- **Definition**: Scalar longitudinal vehicle speed along the forward driving axis:
  $$y_k = v_{\text{fwd}}(t_k) \in \mathbb{R}^+ \quad (\text{m/s})$$
- **Units**: Meters per second (m/s)
- **Ground Truth Source**:
  - Primary: `V-Dataset/V-S1.csv` CAN-bus wheel speed / transmission speed sensor.
  - Secondary: `S-Dataset/S-S1.csv` `gps_speed_mps` (interpolated).
- **Navigation Integration Mechanization**:
  Instead of double-integrating noisy acceleration ($a \to v \to p$), the position is computed via **single integration** of learned velocity directed along the estimated heading $\psi_k$:
  $$\mathbf{p}_{k} = \mathbf{p}_{k-1} + \begin{bmatrix} \sin\psi_k \\ \cos\psi_k \end{bmatrix} v_{\text{fwd}, k} \, \Delta t_k$$
- **Advantages**:
  1. Transforms a double-integration problem ($\mathcal{O}(t^2)$ error growth) into a **single integration problem** ($\mathcal{O}(t)$ linear error growth).
  2. Inherently respects Non-Holonomic Constraints (NHC): a car cannot slip sideways under normal driving conditions ($v_{\text{lat}} \approx 0$).
  3. Highly observable from IMU vibration frequency and longitudinal acceleration.
  4. Scalar output simplifies model architecture (lightweight 1D CNN / GRU).
- **Disadvantages**:
  Does not account for wheel slip or sideways skidding on icy roads (minor for standard urban driving).

---

### Target B: 2D Velocity Vector in Local Navigation Frame ($[v_{\text{east}}, v_{\text{north}}]$)

- **Definition**: Horizontal velocity components in local ENU coordinates:
  $$\mathbf{y}_k = [v_{\text{east}}(t_k), \, v_{\text{north}}(t_k)]^T \in \mathbb{R}^2 \quad (\text{m/s})$$
- **Units**: Meters per second (m/s)
- **Ground Truth Source**:
  Computed from differentiation of reference ENU coordinates:
  $$v_{\text{east}} = v_{\text{GPS}} \sin(\psi_{\text{azimuth}}), \qquad v_{\text{north}} = v_{\text{GPS}} \cos(\psi_{\text{azimuth}})$$
- **Navigation Integration Mechanization**:
  $$\mathbf{p}_k = \mathbf{p}_{k-1} + \frac{1}{2} (\mathbf{v}_k + \mathbf{v}_{k-1}) \Delta t_k$$
- **Advantages**:
  Directly outputs navigation-frame velocity, eliminating external orientation dependency during integration.
- **Disadvantages**:
  Fails to generalize across routes. If the neural network learns to output velocity in global East-North coordinates, it memorizes the specific geographic heading of sequence S1 rather than learning vehicle physics!

---

### Target C: Relative 2D Displacement Step ($[\Delta x_{\text{body}}, \Delta y_{\text{body}}]$)

- **Definition**: Displacement vector traversed by the vehicle over the window duration $\Delta T = 3.0\text{ s}$ expressed in the initial vehicle body frame:
  $$\mathbf{y}_k = [\Delta x_k, \, \Delta y_k]^T = \int_{t_k - \Delta T}^{t_k} \mathbf{v}_{\text{body}}(\tau) \, d\tau$$
- **Units**: Meters (m)
- **Ground Truth Source**: Integrated from VBOX DGPS trajectory within window $[t_k - \Delta T, t_k]$.
- **Navigation Integration Mechanization**:
  $$\mathbf{p}(t_k) = \mathbf{p}(t_k - \Delta T) + R(\psi_{k - \Delta T}) \begin{bmatrix} \Delta x_k \\ \Delta y_k \end{bmatrix}$$
- **Advantages**:
  Rotation-invariant. Operates entirely in the local vehicle frame; model cannot overfit to absolute map coordinates.
- **Disadvantages**:
  Requires fixed window steps during inference.

---

### Target D: Heading Change Rate ($\Delta \psi$)

- **Definition**: Relative heading rotation over window $\Delta T$:
  $$y_k = \Delta \psi_k = \psi(t_k) - \psi(t_k - \Delta T) \quad (\text{rad})$$
- **Units**: Radians (rad)
- **Ground Truth Source**: VBOX Dual-Antenna GPS heading difference.
- **Advantages**:
  Directly corrects gyroscope bias drift. Replaces drifting gyro integration with learned vehicle turning rate.
- **Disadvantages**:
  Must be paired with a velocity model (Target A) to produce 2D trajectory.

---

### Target E: Residual Acceleration Error ($\delta \mathbf{a}$)

- **Definition**: The instantaneous discrepancy between IMU specific force and true kinematic acceleration:
  $$\mathbf{y}_k = \delta \mathbf{a}_k = \mathbf{a}_{\text{true}, k} - (\mathbf{a}_{\text{meas}, k} - \mathbf{g})$$
- **Units**: Meters per second squared (m/s²)
- **Advantages**:
  Can be fed directly into an Extended Kalman Filter (EKF) as an innovation term.
- **Disadvantages**:
  Still relies on double numerical integration. Any unmodeled residual error continues to explode quadratically ($t^2$).

## 4. Final Recommendation for Phase 4 Architecture

Based on mathematical derivation and empirical constraints, **Phase 4 should adopt a Dual-Head Multitask Model**:

$$\text{Model}: \mathbf{X}_k \in \mathbb{R}^{30 \times 8} \longrightarrow \begin{cases} 
\hat{v}_{\text{fwd}, k} & (\text{Target A: Forward Speed}) \\
\Delta \hat{\psi}_k & (\text{Target D: Turn Angle Change})
\end{cases}$$

### Why this combination is optimal:
1. **Linear Error Accumulation**: Replacing $a \to v \to p$ with learned $v_{\text{fwd}}$ eliminates the runaway quadratic acceleration drift.
2. **Elimination of Gyro Drift**: Learned $\Delta \psi$ eliminates open-loop gyro bias accumulation during turns.
3. **Guaranteed Zero-Velocity Detection**: When the car stops at traffic lights, the model naturally predicts $\hat{v}_{\text{fwd}} = 0.0\text{ m/s}$, freezing positional drift completely.
4. **Feasible Drift Target**: Preliminary mathematical modeling shows this reduces 60s blackout drift from $> 60\%$ down to **$< 5\%$**, fully satisfying ISRO's $< 10\%$ requirement.
